Knot Theory and Quandles

Knot theory is a branch of mathematics that studies closed curves in three-dimensional space. Two knots are considered equivalent if one can be continuously deformed into the other without cutting the strand or passing it through itself.1 Many of the properties of Knots can also be studied using algebra. One of the algebraic structures used for this is the quandle, whose axioms correspond to the three Reidemeister moves that preserve knot equivalence.


The following sections introduce the basic ideas of knots and quandles before discussing a family of finite quandles called Sharac(n) quandles. These quandles come from conjugation in symmetric groups and can be used to color knot diagrams. My work focused on computationally constructing examples of Sharac(n) quandles and looking for patterns in their size and structure. From these computations, several interesting patterns appeared and led to a number of open questions.
